## 1. TL;DR

- The owner's proposed mechanism — *a second stream's output additively modifies the first stream's weights, LoRA-style* — is one member of a well-established family. The literature already contains **six distinct families** of "stream A conditions stream B": (F1) **additive weight updates** (LoRA; adapters as module-level additive paths; hypernetwork-generated deltas); (F2) **multiplicative weight modulation** (StyleGAN2 modulated convolution, w′ = s·w, per-sample, with demodulation); (F3) **input-dependent kernel combination** (Dynamic Filter Networks, CondConv — the latter provably equivalent to a mixture of experts); (F4) **feature-wise affine modulation** (FiLM, conditional BN/IN, AdaIN, SPADE, TFiLM, Squeeze-and-Excitation as self-conditioning, GLU gating); (F5) **attention-based conditioning** (cross-attention; TFT's static-covariate context vectors wired into a decoder); (F6) **full weight generation** (HyperNetworks — static and dynamic; hypernetworks for time-series forecasting; input-conditioned *generated LoRA* in 2025–26 work).
- **LoRA is the *youngest* member of this family, not the archetype.** It is a *learned, input-independent* additive update (ΔW = BA, frozen W₀) [S1]. The "weights change per input" behaviour the owner describes is older and has three canonical names: **hypernetworks** (weights generated from a conditioning input, 2016) [S2], **FiLM / feature-wise transformations** (input-dependent affine modulation of activations, 2017–18) [S3][S4], and **dynamic/demodulated convolution** (input-dependent kernels, 2016–2019) [S5][S6][S7].
- **The owner's exact architecture (frequency stream additively modifying time-stream weights) has published precedents at every level of coupling**: feature-level (TFiLM: an RNN stream modulates a conv stream's activations, NeurIPS 2019 [S8]; TFT: static-covariate encoders produce context vectors that condition temporal processing [S9]); weight-level (dynamic hypernetworks generate weights that vary per timestep [S2]; COPRA generates *LoRA weights* conditioned on the input, 2026 [S10]); and band-level frequency conditioning for AD (CATCH: the frequency domain is *patchified into bands* and different anomaly types show up in different bands — shapelet anomalies "mainly affect the third frequency band while seasonal anomalies affect the first two" [S11]).
- **For THIS repo** (JEPA latent-prediction TSAD; no `torch.fft` anywhere in models — verified by grep this session): the cheapest verified mechanism to bring frequency→time conditioning in is **FiLM-style** (frequency summary → per-channel γ,β on the predictor/encoder), the most faithful to the owner's LoRA intuition is a **hypernetwork-generated low-rank ΔW** (COPRA-style [S10]), and the most interpretable for the "specific bands" idea is **CondConv-style routing over band-expert kernels** [S7] or **CATCH-style frequency-band patching** as a scoring signal [S11]. All §6 mapping is design inference.

## 2. The six families of cross-stream conditioning (verified)

| # | Family | What changes | Conditioning source | Canonical exemplar (verified) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Additive weight update | W ← W₀ + ΔW (ΔW learned, input-*independent*) | task/domain, not input | LoRA [S1]; Adapters (module-level additive residual) [S12] |
| F2 | Multiplicative weight modulation | w′ᵢⱼₖ = sᵢ·wᵢⱼₖ per sample (input-dependent) | style/latent vector | StyleGAN2 modulated convolution + demodulation [S13] |
| F3 | Input-dependent kernel combination | kernel = Σᵢ αᵢ(x)·Wᵢ, α = routing fn of input | same or other input | Dynamic Filter Networks [S5], CondConv [S7], MoE (equivalent form) [S14] |
| F4 | Feature-wise affine modulation | h ← γ(x)·h + β(x) (activations, not weights) | other input (or self) | FiLM [S3], CBN/CIN/AdaIN [S4][S15], SPADE [S16], TFiLM [S8], SE [S17] |
| F5 | Attention-based conditioning | values re-weighted by Q–K similarity across streams | other stream's tokens | Cross-attention (TFT static enrichment) [S9], CATCH masked attention [S11] |
| F6 | Full/generated weights | weights (or ΔW) generated by a separate network | any input, incl. per-timestep | HyperNetworks (static + dynamic) [S2], TS hypernetworks [S18][S19], COPRA generated-LoRA [S10] |

Key distinction (verified wording): **F1 changes the weights once, per task** ("During training, W₀ is frozen... A and B contain trainable parameters" [S1]) — it does *not* make behavior input-dependent. **F2–F6 change the effective weights or activations per input sample**, which is the property the owner is actually describing. The Distill survey by the FiLM authors makes the same point for F4: the concatenation of all FiLM coefficients is "both an instruction on *how to modulate* the conditioned network and a *representation* of the task at hand" — the modulation parameters ARE the per-input behaviour [S4].

## 3. Verified details per mechanism

### F1 — Additive weight updates

**LoRA** (Hu et al., 2021; ICLR 2022) [S1]. Verified from the paper (arXiv HTML v2):
- "For a pre-trained weight matrix W₀ ∈ ℝ^(d×k), we constrain its update by representing the latter with a low-rank decomposition W₀ + ΔW = W₀ + BA, where B ∈ ℝ^(d×r), A ∈ ℝ^(r×k), and the rank r ≪ min(d,k)."
- "During training, W₀ is frozen and does not receive gradient updates, while A and B contain trainable parameters."
- Forward pass: "h = W₀x + ΔWx = W₀x + BAx" — the two paths are summed coordinate-wise on the *output* of the same input x. **The update is learned, not generated per input**; the paper's own framing is task adaptation ("low 'intrinsic rank' during adaptation").
- Initialization: "random Gaussian initialization for A and zero for B, so ΔW = BA is zero at the beginning"; scaling "ΔWx by α/r".
- Official code: `microsoft/LoRA` [S1].

**Adapters** (Houlsby et al., ICML 2019) [S12] — the established predecessor of LoRA in the PEFT lineage (LoRA's own citation lists adapters as related work [S1]). Verified from PMLR page + arXiv PDF:
- "Adapters attain near state-of-the-art performance, whilst adding only a few parameters per task. On GLUE, we attain within 0.8% of the performance of full fine-tuning, adding only 3.6% parameters per task."
- Bottleneck module with internal skip connection: "if the parameters of the projection layers are initialized to near-zero, the module is initialized to an approximate identity function."
- The paper explicitly groups conditional normalization, FiLM, and self-modulation as the same parameter-efficient-adaptation family [S12].
- Official code: `google-research/adapter-bert` [S12].

### F2 — Multiplicative weight modulation (per-sample)

**StyleGAN2 modulated convolution** (Karras et al., 2019/2020) [S13]. Verified from the paper (arXiv PDF):
- "The modulation scales each input feature map of the convolution based on the incoming style, which can alternatively be implemented by scaling the convolution weights: w′ᵢⱼₖ = sᵢ·wᵢⱼₖ" — i.e., *input-dependent weight scaling*.
- Per-sample weights break standard primitives: "the resulting set of weights will be different for each sample in a minibatch, which rules out direct implementation using standard convolution primitives. Instead, we choose to employ grouped convolutions."
- Demodulation (Eq. 3) restores unit output statistics, replacing instance normalization; "style modulation may amplify certain feature maps by an order of magnitude or more" without it.
- Result: 40% faster training at 1024² (61 vs 37 img/s on 8×V100) [S13].

### F3 — Input-dependent kernel combination

**Dynamic Filter Networks** (De Brabandere, Jia, Tuytelaars, Van Gool, 2016) [S5]. Verified from arXiv PDF/ar5iv:
- "In a traditional convolutional layer, the learned filters stay fixed after training... we introduce a new framework, the Dynamic Filter Network, where filters are generated dynamically conditioned on an input."
- Architecture: "a filter-generating network that produces filters conditioned on an input, and a dynamic filtering layer that applies the generated filters to another input. **The two inputs of the module can be either identical or different, depending on the task**" — the canonical "stream A generates the kernel that processes stream B" formulation.
- Variants: dynamic convolution (one filter, sample-specific) and dynamic local filtering (position-specific filters).

**CondConv** (Yang, Bender, Le, Ngiam, NeurIPS 2019) [S7]. Verified from arXiv PDF + NeurIPS page:
- "Output(x) = σ((α₁·W₁ + ... + αₙ·Wₙ) ∗ x) where each αᵢ = rᵢ(x) is an example-dependent scalar weight computed using a routing function with learned parameters."
- Equivalence: "By parameterizing the convolutional kernel conditionally on the input, CondConv is mathematically equivalent to the mixture of experts approach, but requires only 1 convolution."
- Result: "CondConv applied to EfficientNet-B0 achieves state-of-the-art performance of 78.3% accuracy with only 413M multiply-adds" on ImageNet.
- Official code: tensorflow/tpu condconv [S7].

**Mixture of Experts** (Shazeer et al., 2017) [S14] — the input-dependent-routing ancestor: "A trainable gating network determines a sparse combination of these experts to use for each example" (verified abstract). CondConv's equivalence claim [S7] is the link.

### F4 — Feature-wise affine modulation

**FiLM** (Perez et al., AAAI 2018) [S3]. Verified from arXiv HTML + AAAI page:
- "FiLM(Fᵢ,𝖼 | γᵢ,𝖼, βᵢ,𝖼) = γᵢ,𝖼·Fᵢ,𝖼 + βᵢ,𝖼" — per-feature-map affine transform.
- "Modulation of a target neural network's processing can be based on the same input to that neural network or some other input, as in the case of multi-modal or conditional tasks."
- Learned behaviour is selective: γ histograms "show a sharp peak at 0, showing that FiLM learns to use the question to shut off or significantly suppress whole feature maps"; 36% of γ values negative; ablations: replacing β with its training mean drops accuracy 1.0%, replacing γ drops it **65.4%** — conditioning is carried mainly by γ (scaling).
- Results: "halve state-of-the-art error for the CLEVR benchmark".
- Official code: `ethanjperez/film` [S3].

**Feature-wise transformations survey** (Dumoulin, Perez, et al., Distill 2018) [S4] — primary-source survey by the FiLM authors: conditional batch norm [S12], conditional instance norm, and AdaIN are all instances of FiLM; FiLM nomenclature is "decoupled from normalization operations". Verified abstract: "we show how they can be conceptualized using the Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) nomenclature".

**AdaIN** (Huang & Belongie, ICCV 2017) [S15]: "aligns the mean and variance of the content features with those of the style features" (verified abstract).

**SPADE** (Park et al., CVPR 2019 oral) [S16]: "a conditional normalization layer that modulates the activations using input semantic layouts through a spatially-adaptive, learned transformation" — per-pixel (per-position) γ,β from a *different* input stream; verified: for spatially-invariant conditioning "we arrive at the form of Conditional Batch Normalization". Motivation directly relevant to dual-stream design: normalization layers "tend to 'wash away' semantic information" when the conditioning is only fed at the input — SPADE injects it at every layer instead. Official code: `NVlabs/SPADE`.

**TFiLM — Temporal FiLM** (Birnbaum, Kuleshov, Enam, Koh, Ermon, NeurIPS 2019) [S8] — the sequence-domain FiLM: "uses a recurrent neural network to alter the activations of a convolutional model", expanding the receptive field of conv sequence models. Mechanism (verified, Algorithm 1): activations F ∈ ℝ^(T×C) are reshaped into blocks, pooled, fed to an LSTM, and the LSTM output provides the modulation. Applications: text classification, audio super-resolution, DNA. This is the closest established "one stream (RNN summary) conditions another stream (conv) in the time dimension".

**Squeeze-and-Excitation** (Hu, Shen, Sun, CVPR 2018) [S17] — self-conditioning: "adaptively recalibrates channel-wise feature responses by explicitly modelling interdependencies between channels" via squeeze (global average pooling → channel descriptor) and excitation (sigmoid gating). "SENets formed the foundation of our ILSVRC 2017 classification submission which won first place... reduced the top-5 error to 2.251%". Verified formula: s = σ(W₂δ(W₁z)).

### F5 — Attention-based conditioning

**Temporal Fusion Transformer** (Lim et al., 2019) [S9]. Verified from arXiv:
- "Static Covariate Encoders – to integrate static features into the network, through encoding of context vectors to condition temporal dynamics."
- Four GRN encoders produce context vectors c_s (variable selection), c_c, c_h (local processing), c_e (enrichment), "wired into various locations in the temporal fusion decoder".
- Gated Residual Network: GRN(a, c) = LayerNorm(a + GLU(η₁)) — a GLU gate decides "the extent to which the GRN contributes to the original input a – potentially skipping over the layer entirely".
- Ablations (average P90 loss increase when removed): gating +1.9%, static covariate encoders +2.6%, instance-wise variable selection +4.1% — the conditioning components are load-bearing [S9].

**CATCH** (Wu et al., 2024–2026) [S11] — attention *inside* frequency bands for AD (see §5).

### F6 — Full/generated weights (hypernetworks)

**HyperNetworks** (Ha, Dai, Le, 2016) [S2]. Verified from arXiv PDF + Google Research page:
- "an approach of using a one network, also known as a hypernetwork, to generate the weights for another network"; trained "end-to-end with backpropagation".
- Static hypernetworks generate conv weights from layer embeddings; **dynamic hypernetworks** generate RNN weights that "can vary across many timesteps" — the original "weights depend on the input/time" mechanism.
- Results: HyperLSTM reaches BLEU 40.03 on WMT En→Fr (single model) vs GNMT ensemble 40.35; conv hypernetworks competitive with fewer parameters [S2].

**Hypernetworks for time series** (Lee et al., 2022) [S18]: "we build a hypernetwork that generates other target models' parameters expected to perform well on the future data" — for temporal drift. **HN-MVTS** (Savchenko & Kachan, 2025 preprint) [S19]: hypernetwork "learns to generate the weights of the last layer of the target forecasting networks", training-only (no inference overhead).

**Generated LoRA (input-conditioned ΔW)** (COPRA, 2026 preprint) [S10]: "The encoder extracts a compact conditioning signal from the input... which is then fed into [a second network] to produce an input-specific parameter update Δ. This formulation enables adaptive, instance-conditioned modulation of the baseline model": Δθ(V) = g(υ(V)), applied as f(V; θ + Δθ(V)). This is exactly the owner's "second stream additively modifies first stream's weights, LoRA-style" — with the ΔW *generated* from the input rather than learned once. Flag: preprint, video-anomaly domain (see §7).

## 4. What the primary sources say about choosing a mechanism

- **Weights vs activations**: modulating activations (F4) costs only 2 parameters per channel per layer ("only 2d parameters per layer" for normalization-parameter tuning [S12]); modulating weights (F2/F3/F6) is more expressive per the StyleGAN2 analysis ("style modulation may amplify certain feature maps by an order of magnitude or more" — needs demodulation [S13]) and costs more compute (per-sample weights rule out standard conv primitives [S13]; CondConv answers with the experts-combined-once trick [S7]).
- **Input-dependent vs task-dependent**: LoRA/adapters change behavior per task, not per input [S1][S12]; for per-input adaptation the verified options are FiLM-family [S3][S8], dynamic filters [S5][S7], or hypernetworks [S2][S10].
- **Where to inject**: SPADE's finding — conditioning only at the network input gets "washed away" by normalization layers; inject at every layer [S16]. TFT's finding — context vectors must be *wired into the right places* (selection, local processing, enrichment) and each placement contributes measurably [S9].
- **Sequence-domain precedent**: TFiLM shows conv+RNN modulation works on 1D sequences with block-wise (temporally local) modulation [S8]; TFT shows static→temporal conditioning works for forecasting [S9].

## 5. Frequency-domain conditioning for anomaly detection (the owner's use case)

The owner's premise — *a single spike should trigger whole-band attention; big periodic anomalies should trigger specific bands* — decomposes into two claims. The **signal-processing half** (an impulse has a broadband spectrum; a periodic signal has energy concentrated at discrete frequencies) is basic DFT duality: **Inference:** (labeled; not a literature claim) — a unit impulse at one timestep has constant-magnitude DFT; a pure sinusoid has a two-line spectrum. The **literature half** is verified:

- **CATCH** (arXiv 2410.12261) [S11] — the strongest verified support for band-specific behavior: "We propose to patchify the frequency domain into frequency bands, which enhances its ability to capture fine-grained frequency characteristics." Verified from the paper's Figure 1 analysis: "shapelet anomalies mainly affect the third frequency band while seasonal anomalies affect the first two frequency bands." Design: FFT → patch/band partition → Channel Fusion Module with a "patch-wise mask generator and a masked-attention mechanism" → separate time-domain and frequency-domain reconstruction scores (time-score, freq-score). Verified: "most algorithms perform poorly when dealing with time series containing high-frequency anomalies, while CATCH demonstrates outstanding performance", including on anomalies "uniformly distributed across each frequency band". (Venue: arXiv v1 2024, revised through 2026; see §8.)
- **FreFilterTST** (Scientific Reports, 2025) [S20]: "Partition the resulting frequency-domain data into five distinct frequency bands. The objective is to then analyze these bands separately to determine how different types of time-domain anomalies manifest as deviations within specific frequency bands." (Search-snapshot; see §8.)
- **TFAD** (CIKM 2022) [S21]: parallel time branch (TCN) + frequency branch (TCN over interleaved Re/Im DFT coefficients) — verified via CATCH's related-work description and the sibling note `RESEARCH_FOURIER_TS_DL.md` §4 [S21].
- **TimeVQVAE-AD** (2023 preprint) [S22]: STFT-based tokenization on a time×frequency grid with "frequency-band anomaly factorization for explainability" — verified in sibling note §5.2; the closest published relative to masked-prediction (JEPA-style) scoring over time–frequency tokens.
- **FITS** (ICLR 2024) [S23]: low-pass filter + complex linear layer; its own AD analysis: on binary/event-style data (SMAP/MSL) "time-domain modeling is preferable as the raw data format is sufficiently compact" — i.e., frequency representation helps periodic monitoring data and hurts impulse-style data. Verified in sibling note §3.2/§3.3.
- **Spectral Residual (SR-CNN)** (KDD 2019) [S24]: FFT → log-amplitude minus smoothed average → iFFT saliency; the production Microsoft detector for spike-like anomalies. Verified in sibling note §5.1.
- **TimesNet** (ICLR 2023) [S25]: FFT → top-k periods → 1D-to-2D reshape for multi-period modeling; verified in sibling note §2.

**Synthesis (Inference:, grounded in the verified sources above):** the literature supports the owner's two-mode intuition as a *spectral-shape* distinction (broadband vs narrowband anomalies) — CATCH demonstrates band-localized signatures for different anomaly types [S11], FITS demonstrates the frequency domain's weakness on event/impulse data [S23], SR-CNN demonstrates a broadband-saliency detector [S24]. A frequency stream that *conditions* (rather than replaces) the time stream is the pattern used by every verified hybrid in the sibling note's §4 table [S21–S25].

## 6. Mapping to THIS repo (ALL of §6 is **Design inference**)

Repo facts used (`AGENTS.md` + grep this session): model wiring goes through registries only — `models.BACKBONE_REGISTRY` (`models/__init__.py`), predictor registry `{"tcn", "gru"}` and anti-collapse registry `("none","sigreg","ema","codebook")` (`models/jepa_core.py`), consumed via `utils/common_config.get_jepa_model(p)`; runs are YAML arms under `configs/jepa/`; stages `pretrain|adapt|score`. Verified by grep: **no `torch.fft`/`rfft`/`irfft` anywhere in this repo's Python** — the models and losses are entirely time-domain (consistent with `RESEARCH_FOURIER_TS_DL.md` §6).

Candidate ways to realize the owner's time-stream + frequency-stream design, ranked by (verified) precedent and expected implementation cost:

1. **FiLM-style frequency conditioning (cheapest; F4).** Frequency summary of the window (e.g., rFFT → magnitude → small MLP → per-channel γ,β) applied to the encoder/predictor activations. Precedent: FiLM [S3], TFiLM for the temporal/blockwise variant [S8], TFT for wiring context vectors into specific places [S9]. Plugs into `BACKBONE_REGISTRY` or a new conditioning module without touching the loss.
2. **CondConv-style band experts (most interpretable; F3).** Partition the spectrum into bands (CATCH's patching [S11]); each band expert is a conv kernel set; a routing function of the frequency summary computes αᵢ; kernel = Σ αᵢWᵢ [S7]. Band experts give the "check specific bands" behaviour directly, with routing weights as an explainability artifact.
3. **Hypernetwork-generated low-rank ΔW (most faithful to the LoRA intuition; F6).** Frequency summary → small hypernetwork → ΔW = BA applied to a time-stream layer; COPRA's Δθ(V) = g(υ(V)) is the direct precedent [S10]; dynamic hypernetworks give per-window variation [S2]. Training cost is the classic hypernetwork caveat (weights must be materialized per sample, as in StyleGAN2's grouped-convolution trick [S13]).
4. **CATCH-style frequency-band scoring (scoring-level; F5/F4).** Add a band-patched frequency reconstruction/consistency score fused at the `Scorer` level alongside the dense-L1 latent-prediction scores — mirrors CATCH's time-score + freq-score fusion [S11] and the sibling note's suggestion of a spectral-residual scoring signal (`RESEARCH_FOURIER_TS_DL.md` §6 item 4).
5. **Period-aware masking (pretext-level).** TimesNet-style top-k period discovery [S25] to bias the existing masked-corpus stage (sibling note §6 item 1) — cheapest way to make the frequency domain influence pretraining without new layers.

Hygiene notes (from sibling notes, verified): keep thresholds clean-train-only (§5.4 of `RESEARCH_FOURIER_TS_DL.md`); any new criterion/backbone name must be registered in the existing factories (`AGENTS.md`); the repo has no `torch.fft` dependency yet — `torch.fft` is first-party PyTorch, no new dependency needed [S26].
